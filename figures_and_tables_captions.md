Figure 1. Water budgets and normalized habitats on common scales. Lines are means and shaded regions ±1 standard deviation across 384 paired parameter draws, not confidence intervals on planetary history. Hot Venus has zero modeled suitable crustal water. Suitable equivalent volume applies a common 0–80 °C score and is not occupied biomass.

Figure 2. Physical delivery and conditional establishment are different quantities. Left: published ten-million-year dynamical fractions from Worth et al. 2013, using present orbital architecture. Right: mean conditional establishment rates with a common one-million exported-carrier normalization per Myr, flat source history and imposed biological factors; values are averaged over the modeled launch ages. Venus-origin and Jovian-origin routes are not calibrated and are excluded from this ranking.

Figure 3. Two-stage Mars to Jovian moon to Venus sensitivity. Colour is the log ratio to direct Mars–Venus transfer under imposed biological factors of 0.01 per stage. The white contour is parity. Neither the unknown return probability nor biological reexport amplification is measured. Wet receiver and functioning moon habitat are necessary conditions; no second-leg orbital calculation is represented.

Archived Table A1. Reference output snapshots. Fractions are normalized by assigned pore water, including portions outside the thermal window; they are not fractions of total planetary water or crust.

Archived Table A2. Illustrative route odds under matched abiogenesis priors and the stated biological transfer priors. This particular construction penalizes the additional transfer filters and yields Bayes factors below one. It does not favor a Martian origin under those assumptions. Changing uncalibrated biological priors can change the comparison; these values are not a measured probability of the location of abiogenesis.

Figure S1. Depth-resolved water-weighted thermal suitability and integrated suitable-water equivalent for the reference endmember. The common heatmap scale is clipped at 0.05 for display; integration uses unclipped values. Grey identifies the adopted LUCA clock interval 19 and green the high-volume Martian field. Pore water, rather than rock volume, is integrated. Neither panel is a map of inhabited volume.

Figure S2. Conserved revised Martian GEL inventory above the earlier Tharsis time series. Unbound, mineral-bound and escaped components partition initial water plus outgassing in the upper panel. The historical lower traces are reproduced from project data and are not observationally inferred total reservoirs. Their record begins at 4.0 Ga. The grey band is the adopted ancestral clock interval 19.

Figure S3. Area-weighted heat flow, near-surface gradient and mean thermally eligible pore-water temperature for the reference columns. At the 4.2 Ga marker the latter is 35.84 °C for Mars and 42.34 °C for Earth. These are conditional volume-weighted means, not paleothermometers or inferred optimal temperatures of LUCA. The grey interval shows clock uncertainty only 19, not uncertainty in the thermal histories.

Figure S4. Published dynamical transfer fractions and an explicitly illustrative early launch chronology. Mars-to-Earth and Mars-to-Venus arrival fractions are 2.59% and 1.52% over 10 Myr, compared with 0.18% for Earth-to-Mars. Median delays are 5.2 and 5.8 Myr for the two Mars-origin destinations in this ensemble 20. Arrows indicate material exchange, not detected organisms or biological inoculation. The launch at 4.36 Ga is a scenario, not a dated event. Receiver histories are schematic and Venus habitability is unresolved.

Figure S5. Conditional seeding feasibility under broad biological priors and the generic dependence of origin odds on prior probability and a stipulated Bayes factor. Values below  are grouped at the left edge of the histogram. The curves at right are sensitivity relations, not Bayes factors inferred from comparative biology. Simulation-count uncertainty is narrower than the unidentified biological uncertainty.

Figure S6. Signed Mars-minus-Earth suitable-water equivalents under three joint thermal, water-inventory and closure endmembers. These are sensitivity scenarios rather than a statistical uncertainty envelope. Correlated parameter changes prevent assigning a single physical cause to differences between the endmembers.

Figure S7. Exchange volumes and rates normalized to 1  expelled from each donor over 10 Myr, with arrow widths proportional to delivered volume under an equal-volume-particle assumption. The source dynamical ensemble supplies particle fractions, not absolute geological fluxes 20. Venus-origin routes are uncalibrated. Composition bars are assumed volume-fraction scenarios for hydrated basalt, fresh basalt and an ultramafic endmember, not inferred lithological percentages. Figure S8. Spherical sections at 3.8 Ga for a 5 m radius and a 5.2 Myr Mars-to-Earth transit. The shell exceeds the chosen 100 kGy absorbed-dose threshold under a radial attenuation surrogate; the labelled percentage is spherical volume, not cross-sectional area. XUV-only skin depth varies with launch age, while the separately stipulated GCR term is held constant. Assumed attenuation lengths and densities are exported with the code. These are neither chemical-conversion percentages nor calibrated survival probabilities 23,24,25.
